# Levels of Consciousness for Marketing & Sales — Summary
Source: 3-day workshop by Scott Oldford (marketing/business) & Frederick Dotson (author of *Levels of Energy*, consciousness researcher). Condensed from the full transcript.

---

## The Core Framework

Frederick's original scale (from *Levels of Energy*) runs low to high:
**Shame, guilt, apathy, grief → fear, craving, anger, overwhelm, arrogance → boredom, courage, neutrality, interest → enthusiasm, positive expectation, power, appreciation → unconditional love, humor, peace, bliss.**

Scott's simplified 4-level version for business/marketing (the one used throughout the workshop):

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Reactive** | Fear/scarcity driven. Lives in crisis mode. No structure, no follow-through. ~80% of the population sits at reactive or low-willful. |
| **Willful** | "I do this, I do this." Motivated by grinding, proving, achieving through sheer effort. Burns out. Most side-hustle/hustle-culture marketing targets this level. |
| **Intellectual** | Strategic, data-driven, systems-and-frameworks minded. Best level for building a scalable, profitable business — but often unfulfilling; can feel like "success on paper, empty inside." |
| **Intuitive** | Presence-based, feeling-led, low attachment to outcome. Highest profit margins (least energy loss) but hardest to scale — has a natural revenue ceiling unless paired with intellectual structure. |

Nobody lives permanently in one level. The goal isn't to "ascend and stay" — it's to know which level a task requires and consciously shift into it (e.g. willful to grind out a launch, intellectual to plan finances, intuitive to create content, and back again).

**Key principle: you attract the level you embody.** A business's marketing, sales, team, and delivery should match one consistent level — mismatches (e.g. reactive-style scarcity marketing selling to an intellectual audience) actively repel the right customers.

## Frederick's Teaching (Days 1–2): Raising Your Own Level

**Three ways to raise consciousness:**
1. **Authenticity** — Know when you say yes but mean no (and vice versa). Drop "secret contracts" (unspoken expectations) in favour of openly stating wants. Set real boundaries. Practice silent integrity (doing good without publicising it) over expressed virtue (loudly signalling goodness).
2. **Integrity** — Doing what you know is true/good vs. what's expedient in the moment.
3. **Practising higher emotions** — Deliberately generating states like appreciation, humour, presence, rather than waiting for external circumstances to produce them.

**The "liquid wall" exercise**: imagine walking through a wall of liquid between two realities/states — everything you feel on one side gets "sucked up" and left behind as you emerge on the other. Used repeatedly through the workshop to physically/imaginatively shift state before doing anything important (marketing, launch planning, big decisions).

**On money & value**: "If you know the formula for gold, you don't have to carry a bag of gold around." Success and money follow from consistently providing real value/skill — not from chasing money directly. Price is largely arbitrary and contextual (his own example: went from an $800 top offer to a $90,000 booking simply by pricing a $5M "premium" tier as a contrast anchor). Affluent buyers are drawn to exclusivity, self-improvement, and things they can't get everywhere — not to being sold to.

**Good marketing = giving a real experience, not promising one.** Fascination/beauty/uniqueness outperforms benefit-stacking. The highest form of marketing is "marketing that isn't recognized as marketing" — earned attention through genuinely good work, word of mouth, and content people already want, not funnels or ads.

## Scott's Business Framework (Days 1–3): Applying Levels to Every Part of the Business

Scott mapped each of the four levels (reactive/willful/intellectual/intuitive) across every function of a business. Condensed by function below.

### Marketing & Funnels
- **Reactive/Willful**: Highly structured, step-by-step funnels (video sequences, 5-day challenges). Scarcity, urgency, pain/consequence framing. Deliberately unpolished/rugged design — too-polished pushes this audience away. High repetition needed; the worse the product, the more repetition it needs to sell.
- **Intellectual**: Case studies, data, frameworks, long-form content (5,000–10,000 word posts), processes. Less structured — give the full picture and let them self-pace. Skeptical by default; wants proof.
- **Intuitive**: No real funnel exists. Content is created only when genuinely inspired — forcing a content calendar kills it. Aesthetic is about frequency/alignment, not polish. Attraction happens slowly, sometimes over years, through consistently good, authentic content — not calls to action.

### Sales
- **Reactive**: Needs to be made to react — fear, urgency, "buy now or else."
- **Willful**: Needs a transformation narrative — pain → vision → gap → commitment (Scott's own 4-step sales process, originally built for this level).
- **Intellectual**: Needs reasons, data, case studies, and a rational scarcity ("only 6 spots this month because of my calendar") — not manufactured urgency.
- **Intuitive**: No persuasion. "Here's what it is, let me know when it's time." Buys on feeling/timing, not argument. Barely needs a phone call — decisions happen via presence/trust, sometimes just text.

### Team & Operations
- Every level of consciousness plays a needed operational role: reactive/willful people are the "doers" who get high-volume tasks done (customer service, outreach); intellectual people build and run the systems, oversight, and finance; intuitive people set vision and culture.
- Mismatches break things — e.g. an intuitive person doing bookkeeping, or a willful person left unsupervised on finances, both create chaos.
- A business (and its owner) usually needs a *blend* of all three to avoid burnout (willful alone), boredom/rigidity (intellectual alone), or an inability to scale (intuitive alone).

### Finances
- **Reactive**: Can't hold money — no belief structure that more is possible; relies on luck/inheritance.
- **Willful**: Makes money then immediately re-spends/reinvests out of fear of stopping — avoids ever having a stable reserve.
- **Intellectual**: Best at *keeping* and structuring money (projections, budgeting) but rarely makes outsized/exponential returns — too conservative/data-bound for high-risk, high-reward moves.
- **Intuitive**: Highest-return decisions often come from intuition with no supporting data (Scott's Bitcoin-at-$7 example) — but intuitive people also care less about money as they progress, which can cap growth unless they consciously re-engage intellectual planning.

### Business Model / Profitability
- Profit = energy left over after "optimization." The more aligned every part of the system (marketing, team, operations) is with the intended consciousness level, the less energy is lost — and the higher the margin.
- Intellectual-consciousness businesses are, in Scott's experience, the easiest to scale past 7–8 figures because they're the most systemizable.
- Intuitive-consciousness businesses have the *highest* profit margins per dollar (least energy waste) but the *lowest* ceiling unless deliberately built on an intellectual operating layer underneath.

## Practical Selling-Language Examples (from the workshop)
Same message, four levels:

- *Toilet paper shortage*: Reactive — "Stock up now, the end is near." / Intellectual — "Buy 3, get 1 free." / Intuitive — "Choose whichever toilet paper you like" (framed as their own free choice).
- *Weight loss*: Reactive — "You need these pills" (repeated). / Intellectual — "These pills have the right ingredient combination — test them yourself." / Intuitive — "What do you believe is required to lose weight?"
- *Home security*: Reactive — fear ("you're in danger without it"). / Intellectual — "Control your security remotely, try it free." / Intuitive — "Have you thought about home security?" (No pressure either way.)
