# Prospect Avatars — Heart Attractor

> Real prospect profiles. Use these when writing Reel headlines, captions, emails, and any content where a specific person needs to be held in mind. Content must speak to *these* people — not a generic manifestation audience.

---

## Primary avatar — Rachel

**Always write to Rachel first.** She is the creative anchor for all content. Full profile in `avatar-rachel.md`.

Rachel is a seeker. She has invested deeply in manifestation. She believes it works. She cannot make it work for her love life. The primary reason: she has Internal Working Models — unconscious beliefs about her own worthiness and whether others can be trusted, formed in childhood — running the show. "I'm not worthy," "I have to earn love," "closeness is dangerous." These are what's actually driving the chasing, fixating and self-doubt that push love away. She wants a life partner — someone to live and enjoy her life with.

**The niche is attracting a new life partner.** The audience is people who want this specific outcome, not a broad self-improvement journey — searching things like "how to manifest love," "how to attract my soulmate," "why do I keep attracting the same type of partner."

## Avatar 1 — The Frustrated Manifestor

The primary avatar. Believes in manifestation completely. Has tried the techniques. Still single.

### Who they are
- Deeply invested in manifestation — vision boards, scripting, affirmations
- Believes attracting a relationship is possible — has seen it work in other areas of life
- Has spent money on courses, books and coaches promising "the One" that didn't deliver
- Still single, or still repeating the same pattern with the wrong people

### Promised Land
Living and enjoying their life with their amazing partner.

### Bleeding Neck Pain
Watching someone they want pull away in real time — and knowing, in that moment, it's their own neediness pushing it away.

### False Belief
That they just need the right technique, to be more consistent with affirmations and scripting, or to just stay positive.

### What they've tried
- Vision boards and scripting
- Affirmations repeated on faith
- Joe Dispenza meditations, Neville Goddard techniques, Abraham Hicks / Law of Attraction
- Various courses, books, and coaches promising "the One"

### Direct quotes from this avatar
- "I've tried everything and I'm still single"
- "I know it's supposed to work this way. I just can't make it land."

## Avatar 2 — The Chaser

Gets fixated on one person and can feel their own neediness pushing them away, in real time, and can't stop it.

### Who they are
- Overanalyses every text, every interaction, every silence
- Knows chasing pushes people away — can't stop doing it anyway
- The bleeding neck pain avatar — this is the moment the whole framework is built around

### Promised Land
Staying regulated and secure without performing indifference. Attracting interest without needing to chase it.

### Bleeding Neck Pain
Watching someone they want pull away in real time — and knowing it's their own neediness doing it.

### False Belief
That if they could just detach and stop caring so much, it would stop happening.

## Avatar 3 — The Detacher

Took "just let go and stop caring" advice too far. Now stuck between forcing and giving up entirely.

### Who they are
- Burned out from chasing, tried the opposite: detach completely, "focus on themselves," stop looking
- Privately still wants the relationship badly
- Worried that even wanting it "too much" is what's blocking it

### Promised Land
Letting go without it meaning giving up. Wanting the relationship openly, without fear that wanting it is the problem.

### Bleeding Neck Pain
Fear that focusing on being single reinforces being single. The quiet dread that "not caring" isn't working either.

### False Belief
That detaching and not caring is the fix — the opposite failure mode to the Chaser, same root cause.

## Avatar 4 — The Repeat Pattern

Keeps attracting the same type of emotionally unavailable or mismatched partner.

### Who they are
- Understands attachment theory, subconscious patterns
- Has done therapy, read the books — intellectually
- Keeps attracting the same type of person, or the same arguments, or the same loneliness

### Promised Land
A relationship that feels genuinely safe and real. Attracting someone different — not just trying to choose differently.

### Bleeding Neck Pain
"Why do I keep attracting the same type of partner?" — asked and never answered.

### False Belief
That knowing the pattern should have been enough to change it.

## Avatar 5 — The Self-Doubter

Carries quiet unworthiness beliefs. Wonders if they're even capable of being loved the way they want.

### Who they are
- Outwardly confident, successful, put-together
- Privately doubts whether they deserve the relationship they want
- Feels foolish for believing in something that seems "woo"

### Promised Land
Receiving interest, affection and commitment without flinching or sabotaging it.

### Bleeding Neck Pain
Sabotaging good things right as they start to work. Terrified of watching another year pass while everyone else moves on.

### False Belief
That they just need to feel more confident, or that they're simply not ready yet.

## Audience Consciousness Level

**Willful, with some Intellectual** — per the Scott Oldford / Frederick Dotson levels-of-consciousness framework (not the Hawkins scale used for the main Heart Creator positioning). This audience wants:
- A clear transformation story, not a mechanism explanation
- One simple path, not multiple options
- Real, visible proof over data or theory
- Direct, outcome-first language — "attract your person," not abstract psychological framing

**In practice this means:**
- Content is emotional but not pain-focused
- Lead with emotional recognition (brief and real), follow with a simple, concrete explanation — not neuroscience detail
- The reader should feel recognised as capable — not rescued
- Avoid content that speaks primarily to despair, apathy or crisis

## What All Five Have in Common

### The Shared Journey

1. **They want a relationship and it isn't happening** — either not at all, or not in a way that lasts
2. **They discovered manifestation** — believed it could work for love the way it's worked elsewhere
3. **They went all in** — vision boards, scripting, affirmations, courses, coaches
4. **It didn't stick** — hope followed by heartbreak, or nothing at all
5. **They are stuck between forcing and detaching** — neither approach has worked, and they don't know why

They are not sceptics. They are **believers stuck at the door.**

### The Core Pattern

Not a technique problem. Not a consistency problem. Not a caring-too-much or caring-too-little problem. Not a positivity problem.

Internal Working Models — unconscious beliefs about their own worthiness and whether others can be trusted, formed in childhood — are running the show. "I'm not worthy," "I have to earn love," "closeness is dangerous." These are what's actually driving the chasing, the fixating, the self-doubt that push love away.

### The Unasked Questions They Carry Silently

- "Why does everyone else seem to find this so easy?"
- "Why do I know exactly what I'm doing wrong and still can't stop doing it?"
- "Am I the reason this keeps happening?"
- "Is this just not going to happen for me?"
- "How much longer can I keep trying before I give up?"

### What They Actually Want

Specific, ordinary moments they can picture:

- Waking up next to someone who chose them
- Not checking their phone anxiously, waiting to hear back
- Feeling calm instead of bracing when things go quiet
- Trusting themselves not to sabotage it

**When writing content, name one of these specific moments rather than the abstract concept.**

### What They're Wary Of

- Another programme that becomes just more information
- Overpromised results that don't last
- Being sold to before being understood
- Feeling manipulated or patronised
