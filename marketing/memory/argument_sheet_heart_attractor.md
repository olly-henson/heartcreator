## The Argument

**1. Who**
Growth-minded people who want a lasting relationship and are tired of being told what to fix.

**2. What they want (promised land)**
A committed relationship that feels safe, easy and genuine, with someone they can fully be themselves with, who they're physically attracted to and who's fun and playful.

**3. Pain**
They've worked on themselves and changed other areas of their life. But they still feel held back in love, while friends who never did any of that work are happily settled.

**4. Bleeding neck pain**
A part of them doesn't see it ever happening.
*(From real member words: a cancelled trial member's "I don't see that it will ever happen".)*

**5. Old belief (name it out loud)**
*"I need to find the right method to fix myself, and then I'll be ready to meet my person."*
So they wait, and move from method to method trying to get "ready".

**6. The crack (let them see it for themselves)**
"Think about your friends in happy relationships. How many of them fixed themselves first? Most just met someone."
*(From Catrin: "none of my friends in happy long-term relationships had to consciously do all of that.")*

**7. Analogy**
"You didn't fix every flaw before making your best friend. You just showed up as you were."
Other analogies must be just as simple and come from the avatar's own life (friendships, family, work, yoga, a habit they've already built). Best of all: a change they've already made somewhere else through practice.

**8. Real cause**
Nothing's wrong with them. They just don't believe they're enough as they are, so they keep looking for something to fix. And that belief isn't something you find in a method. It's a skill you practise every day.

**9. Solution: The Attraction Formula Meditation**
This isn't another way to fix yourself, because nothing needs fixing. It's 10 minutes a day practising believing you're enough and that it can happen, while you get on with your life and dating as you are. Each day you:
- **Regulate:** practise feeling calm and safe, so doubt and fear quieten.
- **Rewrite:** practise believing something new about yourself in place of the old stories from past relationships.
- **Rehearse:** practise how it feels to be with your person, until it feels familiar and real.

**10. New belief (the big domino)**
"I don't need to fix myself to meet my person. I'm already enough. I just need to practise believing it, every day."

**11. Proof**
"After my 7-year relationship ended, I tried everything to fix myself. Neville Goddard, Joe Dispenza, Abraham Hicks. None of it lasted. What changed was when I stopped trying to fix myself and just focused on making myself feel good, every day. That daily practice became this meditation, and it's how I met my wife."
Add members' early wins as they're posted.

**What to expect (keep in content and onboarding)**
Belief is a skill, so nobody is good at it on day one. You feel different first, then you act differently, then who you meet changes. Doubt along the way is normal.

## Why this version (notes from the rebuild)

- **Rebuilt step by step with Olly on 2026-10-16.** The old belief became "fix myself + the right method → then I'll be ready". The crack is Olly's friends point. The real cause is "nothing's wrong with you, you just don't believe you're enough".
- **Practise, never fix (the key wording rule).** Saying "you don't need to fix yourself" and then selling a meditation that fixes you would turn it into one more method. So the meditation is daily practice in believing you're enough, done *while* living and dating as you are. Use *practise, train, strengthen, build*. Avoid *fix, clear, heal, remove blocks, get ready*. Going to the gym doesn't mean your body is broken.
- **Friends and family didn't do the inner work (Olly, 2026-10-16).** Most people's friends and family met their person without doing any inner work. Use it as the main crack against "I need to fix myself first".
- **Churn evidence:** a cancelled trial member said her reason was "my own lack of trust and belief that this will help me attract my partner". She then listed other methods to try and admitted she hadn't practised consistently. Catrin values content about "how to know it's working / what's normal", which no one else makes. The "What to expect" section exists for both. Details: memory note `project_trial_churn_belief`.
- **Belief-shifting needs two parts, every time (added 2026-10-16, from a Csabi Berger article Olly shared).** (1) Name the old belief out loud. (2) Show the crack: examples from their own experience or an analogy that let them conclude "hold on, this doesn't add up". Just stating the new belief ("belief is a skill") gets "so true!" comments and changes nothing, because content that matches what people already think gets agreed with and ignored. The real obstacle isn't wanting a relationship. It's loyalty to a solution that doesn't work (method-hopping).
- **One current belief only.** They think belief comes *from* finding the right method. The truth is that belief comes from daily practice. Same goal, different source: that's the whole turn.
- **Lack of belief stays out of the Who.** People don't describe themselves that way. It's the reveal in stage 8.
- **Belief is a skill.** That's what makes a daily practice the logical answer, and it reframes quitting early as stopping practice too soon.
- **The cause is internal: belief in themselves.** Higher-consciousness members (Willingness to Intellectual) recognise the doubt as their own ("I have to earn love"). Lower, reactive people look outward (another method, a retreat, a therapist). Naming it as internal speaks to the people Olly wants and filters out shiny-object sign-ups. Based on two members so far, so keep testing it against new members' words.
- **Don't frame it as "work".** It's one simple daily practice. The audience is tired of self-improvement homework.
- **Not "meditate and wait".** The practice builds belief, belief changes what you do, and what you do brings the evidence. Never imply people should avoid dating until they believe.
- **Audience level:** a wide band for Skool volume. Courage at minimum, ideally Willingness to Intellectual.
